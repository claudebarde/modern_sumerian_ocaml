# Modern Sumerian — Neologism Glossary

**Last updated:** 4 September 2026

## Orthography

`š` → **sh**, `ḫ` → **h**, `ŋ` → **ĝ**. No vowel diacritics, except where distinctive —
**è** "to leave" against **e** "house". The glottal stop is written with an apostrophe.
Verb forms, case suffixes and enclitics are all written phonemically, without hyphens
between morphemes. Cuneiform uses `·` as a word delimiter; it marks word boundaries
only, so case suffixes and enclitics stay attached to their host.

## Web and interface

Vocabulary for websites and online services: accounts and authentication, messaging,
and the controls and sections of a user interface. All 28 entries accepted as of
4 September 2026.

Notes:
- *kur* "to enter" is homonymous with *kur* "mountain, foreign land", and *kura*
  "enter!" with *kura* "in the mountain". The cuneiform distinguishes them —
  𒆭 against 𒆳 — but Latin text does not.
- *musara* is always written with the final *-ra*; bare *musar* is a different
  attested word ("inscription-maker").
- *kiĝgia* and *unadug* coexist: *kiĝgia* is the general "message, mail" and the
  preferred form for the website interface; *unadug* is specifically "letter".
- The save button *ĝaranib* carries the locative prefix {ni}, which points back
  at *iduba* "in the granary"; the login button *kura* has no such prefix.
- *zir* is "cancel" in the sense of breaking off or aborting an action; *keshed* is
  "close", dismissing a window without aborting anything. Cancelling a standing
  arrangement (a subscription) is a third word, not yet settled.
- *niĝgub* is both singular and plural. Sumerian non-human nouns are not inflected
  for number, and the plural clitic {enē} is human-only.
